Stage 2 · pick one
Four directions, three screens each
Same copy, same layout, same price series in every row — only the design language changes. Each row is lobby, strategy, position, left to right.
Terminal
after Hyperliquid
Near-black with one mint accent, dense rows, monospaced numbers. Reads as an instrument: the number is the product and nothing competes with it.
Costs: Unforgiving to a beginner. Looks like something you need a licence to operate — the opposite of what onboarding promises.
Desk
after Bybit
Exchange grammar: charcoal, gold accent, green and red up-down, tight radii, badges everywhere. Instantly legible to anyone who has used an exchange.
Costs: Familiar to the 5% who already trade, invisible to everyone else. Gold plus green plus red is three accents fighting for the same eye.
Calm
after Robinhood
Black, one green, enormous type and space. One thing per screen, a hand-sized button. The direction that fits "three minutes to your first trade".
Costs: Space costs rows: the strategy list and the trade detail get tight. Green as both accent and "up" makes the neutral state read as profit.
Paper
after Monad
Off-white paper with Monad purple, geometric type, generous corners. The only light direction and the only one that says which chain this is without a logo.
Costs: Light screens are worse at 2am and worse under a bar light. Purple carries no market meaning, so up and down need their own colours anyway.
