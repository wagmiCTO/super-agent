## Maker entry, priced against a 50 notional

A resting entry is charged at the maker rate, and on Perpl the exit is free either way,
so a maker entry looks nearly free — until the per-order posting fee is priced in.
That fee is a flat amount, so it hurts small positions most: it is what decides whether
a limit-order mechanic is viable at the stake our players actually use.

## Leverage

## Caveats

- Volatility is measured on **testnet**. Its price feed tracks the real market, but its
  order book does not: spread and depth are thinner than mainnet, so a market order
  pays slippage these numbers do not include. Slippage is a second cost on top of the
  fee and it hits the same short horizons hardest.
- The break-even column is the horizon at which the *expected* move covers the fee.
  It is a coin flip at that point, not a profit: half the outcomes are still losses.
  A mechanic needs a comfortable multiple of it, not equality.
- The posting fee is a per-order charge on the testnet deployment; mainnet reports it
  as zero on every market. Do not design a limit-order mechanic around the testnet value.
- Funding is excluded. It is a holding cost, so it works against long horizons exactly
  where the fee argument favours them.
